They took a picture of space. They took a picture of collapsing nebulae swirling around distant white dwarf stars. They took a picture of twin galaxies performing interstellar gymnastics, a violent cosmic cartwheel. They took a picture of us. They took a picture of nothing.

Before we could take pictures, we made paintings. We brushed wet pigment across parchment, we made portraits of satellites as their image reflected and bent through warped glass. We tried to make sense of what all this is, we are still trying to make sense of what all this is.

What is all this?

We sent a golden record on a voyage into the infinite expanse. On it, images, sounds, greetings in languages living and dead, music. A guitar riff that captures exactly how it feels to be (really) alive.

## Breath of the Gods

What are the Blind Eternities? To answer that question, I want you first to name something in this photograph.

In 1704, Isaac Newton wrote Opticks, or a Treatise of the Reflexions, Refractions, Inflections and Colours of Light. In it, he proposed a new idea of “aether” – a substance that could bend light, a dense medium in the celestial emptiness between planets and stars. He borrowed the term from Greek myth, where aether was the quintessence, the fifth element, the air breathed by gods. Two hundred and one years later, Albert Einstein satisfied Newton’s curiosity with a theory of special relativity. The concept of aether is no longer useful in modern physics.

When a planeswalker summons a creature in Magic, they pull it from the aether, the chaotic energy of the Blind Eternities. But the creature is not a living entity – it is a copy, a concept. In Chapter 2 of the early Magic novel The Eternal Ice, the necromancer Lim-Dûl explains to Jodah:

> “When you cast the spell, you envisioned that which you would to create, a perfect ‘chair’ that you were trying to emulate. I have heard, and I believe, that there is an ultimate ‘chair’’ somewhere that we both model our thoughts from – one that has the basics of all ‘chair-ness’.

This is Plato’s Theory of Forms. Platonic realism argues for the difference between ordinary physical objects and abstract universals existing outside of human minds. The Ur-Dragon and Arahbo, Roar of the World are both extraordinary entities because they are the avatars, the ideal forms. They are depicted by necessity in these illustrations, but these are approximations, and are merely representational. Against the avatars, all the other dragons and cats are just shadows dancing on the walls of a cave.

## The Perfect Realm

What are the Blind Eternities?

To answer that question, I want you first to think of a color you’ve never seen. The philosophers of Meletis are a privileged class. Formalists in this region study aether. They believe it connects to a realm of abstract forms such as numbers, concepts, and theories – symbols of perfection.

They believe in unsummoning. Everything that is real is necessarily flawed, thus, to unsummon means to return an imperfect object back to its flawless domain. To unsummon is a valorant favor. Plato’s influence permeates the world-building of Theros.

On Kaladesh, aether can be seen, touched, harvested. Flooded rice fields beneath wispy clouds twist into swirling patterns under its magnetic and magnificent influence. Here, aether is an abundant source of energy. It inspires artistry and craftsmanship and invention. On this plane, the essence of a hostile and untenable space becomes a spring-well for ingenuity and even new forms of life.

In 1980, Carl Sagan starred in Cosmos. He reminded us that we are made of star stuff, that we are the children of red giants. The web fiction titled Renewal echoes his message.

> “But the space between those realms, the thing that connects each of those universes, is the same substance that makes up the aetherborn. What makes up you extends far beyond Kaladesh. You are what connects the Multiverse."

## A Hand Into Water

What are the Blind Eternities?

To answer that question, I want you first to consider where you were before you were born. The Eldrazi invaded Zendikar – three titans of incomprehensible power. Like Langoliers, they devour planes and leave geometric trails of bismuth and bone in their horrific wake. They are apathetic to this destruction. They find it necessary to maintain the balance. To understand the Eldrazi is to begin to understand the Blind Eternities, their home.

In the web fiction titled “Revelation at the Eye,” the Elder Dragon Ugin invites Jace to imagine a fish swimming in a pond. When a hand reaches below the surface, the fish is greeted by a five-headed monster. The fingernails and knuckles remain completely alien to the gills and scales of the fish, who mistakes the hand as the aberrations entirety. What remains unseen past the water’s surface is beyond its comprehension.

Like the images from the Webb telescope, which have been shifted from infrared to the visible spectrum, the Eldrazi are projections and extensions of the Blind Eternities. The form that appears to the residents of the planes they invade is not their true form, but rather that which can be interpreted by the citizens’ limited faculties. This is not Emrakul, it is the translation of Emrakul, and the rest of her is somewhere, elsewhere.

In a letter to Sebastiano Timpanaro from July 7th, 1970, Italo Calvino postulated that “Man is simply the best chance we know of that matter has had of providing itself with information about itself… it is the memory of matter that organizes itself through man” In this view, humans are merely the vessel through which the material world became self-aware. This self-awareness defines the phenomena of existence.

The Eldrazi underscore the limits of our sensory systems. We cannot conceive of their true form, and must rely on visual symbols and abstract metaphors to understand them. The Eldrazi parallel the discourse on metaphysics and ontology, or, the study of reality.

Metaphysics takes the inquiries of children, such as “what is a thought?” and “how do I know I am awake?” and addresses them seriously. Metaphysics encourages challenging the most basic and innocuous ideas like “what does it mean to change?” and “are humans free?” Metaphysics understands that the simpler the question, the more difficult the answer. The most fundamental call-and-response of this discipline is: “what is there? and what is it like?”

What is there? And what is it like?

What is the Blind Eternities? And what is it like?

## Paradox & Metaphor

The Blind Eternities are the epitome of paradox. In order to imagine the unimaginable, or to describe the indescribable, we must rely on metaphor. To extend beyond the limits of language and ideas requires using familiar language and ideas. Conceding to the paradox, and having a sense of humor, is the only way to make progress.

The Blind Eternities are not a space, and they are not space. They are not like planes, which can be spherical or flat expanses or entire planets or tiny, self-contained worlds. They are not visible or tangible, but when traveling through them, they become visible and tangible and populated with vistas and colors and shapes.

The first issue of Magic’s BOOM! comic shows how The Blind Eternities shapeshift in relation to their visitor. When Ral Zarek planeswalks, he sees a boundless machine filled with cogs and gears and electricity. When Kaya planeswalks, she sees ghosts. When Vraska planeswalks, she sees death. Once again, these illustrations are just representations, approximations to the idea.

Mark Rosewater describes The Blind Eternities not as a hallway, but a door frame. Doug Beyer describes The Blind Eternities as a “betweenness” that lies in the existential corridors connecting realities. I like that word, it’s weird. Reddit user xenozfan2 describes the multiverse as an apartment complex, where

> “every apartment has no doors and touches every other apartment in the building. The apartments are planes, with every one being completely separate and different from every other… Some have one single room, and some have many. All apartments are separated by walls that you can’t pass through unless you have an ignited spark. No one lives in the walls because they’re not rooms.”

In this metaphor, The Blind Eternities are the two-dimensional space between the walls. And the Eldrazi are rats. The Blind Eternities are an unforgiving tempest of aether, the essence of creation. You can pass through them, but you cannot stay long.

What you may hear in the moments spent traveling between worlds might sound like every track of the Voyager record played at the exact same time. The Blind Eternities may be decorated with rainbows of unseen colors. They may smell like dry clouds, or the blazing sun, or nothing. No matter how the journey unravels, it will be an illusion, a translation of the inconceivable.

We are the fish in the pond. We witness our world through our senses, and they are often untrusty narrators.

## The Path of the Stars

If you were to ask a farmer of Kessig to describe aether and The Blind Eternities, they may respond using metaphors of God or an omniscient creator. If you were to ask an artificer of Kamigawa, they may respond with metaphors of a machine – a neural network, a hard disk, an artificial intelligence capable of infinitely creating and destroying data. If you were to ask an expedition leader of Zendikar, they may respond with metaphors of a nightmare.

The Blind Eternities represent the horizon of our understanding. Like the images pouring in from the Webb telescope, The Blind Eternities challenge our place in a universe that becomes increasingly complex and mysterious and inexplicable. Every new development in our observational tools opens new pathways for understanding – and confusion.

Matter certainly likes to show off. It tempts us like a siren to stay searching for new ways to describe its tricks. The Blind Eternities hold up a curtain to our existence and remind us that we will never understand what is on the other side. And yet, we keep looking.
